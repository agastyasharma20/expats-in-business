## Resident or non-resident

For foreign-exchange rules a person is **resident in India** if they have lived in India for **more than 182 days in the previous financial year**, with exclusions based on the purpose of the stay. The type of account follows from this.

## Which accounts

- **NRE accounts** are only for **Non-Resident Indians and persons of Indian origin**. A foreigner of non-Indian origin cannot open one.
- **NRO accounts** are open to **any person resident outside India**, including a **foreign national of non-Indian origin who is visiting India**. When such a visitor leaves, the balance can be paid out only if the account is **no more than six months old** and has had no local credits except interest.
- **A foreigner living in India** normally uses a resident rupee account. The current RBI direction does not spell out this right in one sentence, so ask the bank. It does let a foreigner who leaves India after a job **re-designate the resident account as an NRO account** and send genuine dues abroad after taxes, **up to US$1 million a financial year**, after which the account is closed.
- A foreign citizen resident in India and employed by an Indian company may keep an **account abroad** to receive or send their entire salary.
- Pakistani nationals need **prior RBI approval**.

## Documents (KYC)

- A **valid identity document**: passport, driving licence or proof of an Aadhaar number are listed.
- **Proof of address:** for foreign nationals, a **letter from the foreign embassy or mission in India** or a document from a foreign government department is accepted.
- **PAN** or **Form 60**.
- **Foreign students** can open an NRO account on a passport with visa and immigration endorsement. The local address must be declared and checked within **30 days**; until then incoming money is capped at US$1,000 and total credits at Rs. 50,000.
- Banks refresh KYC every **2, 8 or 10 years** depending on risk.

## Sending money

- A foreign citizen who is resident but **not permanently resident** (here for a job of no more than three years) may send abroad **up to net salary** after taxes and provident fund.
- For other income, they approach the RBI through their bank.
- The **Liberalised Remittance Scheme** lets **resident individuals** remit up to **US$250,000 per financial year**; **PAN is mandatory**. The scheme text says nothing specific about foreign nationals, so ask your bank whether it applies to you.

## Check before you rely on this

RBI directions are updated often. Confirm your case with your bank, and with the RBI through your bank if it concerns income other than salary.
