## What a bank must collect from you

Under the customer due diligence rules in force since **22 October 2026**, a bank asks for:

- your full name as in a **valid identification document**, such as a **valid passport**;
- your **nationality**, **permanent address** and a **residential address** supported by a **utility bill not over three months old**;
- your **Tax Identification Number (TIN)**;
- for **non-resident customers**, the **reason for opening the account** and a copy of your **valid visa or permit**.

Records for non-residents can be authenticated by a Sri Lankan diplomatic or consular officer, a registrar of companies, or a lawyer or notary.

## The TIN comes first

**From 1 November 2026** you must show a valid **TIN certificate** to open a bank account. Get your TIN first ([how](/guides/lk-tax-tin-and-vat)).

## Foreign investment money

The Board of Investment says investors may deal directly with banks and that **free transfers are allowed through Inward Investment Accounts**. Opening an Inward Investment Account is optional in its approval process.

## Cash you carry

Declare to Customs, with a receipt issued, amounts above **US$15,000** (or the equivalent) when **arriving** and above **US$10,000** when **departing**. The money must be legally acquired.

## Check before you rely on this

The Central Bank's own pages could not be opened during our check, so we **cannot confirm** which foreign-currency accounts a resident or non-resident foreigner may hold, or the official limits for sending money abroad. Ask your bank, and read the Central Bank's Foreign Exchange Act directions on cbsl.gov.lk before you transfer large amounts.
