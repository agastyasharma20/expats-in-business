Polish banks, branches of foreign banks, credit institutions and credit unions (SKOK) that offer payment accounts to consumers must offer a **basic payment account** in Polish złoty. A SKOK only has to offer it to its own members.

## Who it is for

Consumers who **do not already have another złoty payment account** in Poland that allows the same transactions. If your earlier basic account was closed and you can show the provider's notice of closure, holding it is no longer a reason to refuse you (a rule added in 2026).

## If you are a foreigner

- The right is **not limited to Polish or EU citizens**. It covers consumers who are **legally staying in an EU member state**.
- A bank **may refuse** if you do not stay legally in an EU member state, have no **valid identity document**, or have no address of residence or correspondence in Poland. It **must refuse** if there is a money-laundering or crime suspicion, or if you appear on a sanctions list or the KNF warning list.
- The Act asks only for a **valid identity document** and does not name a type. You also have to be able to show that your stay is legal. The Act does not say how, so ask the bank which documents it accepts.
- Whether a person **without a residence permit** counts as legally staying is not settled by the Act. Ask the bank, and if it refuses, ask for the reasons.
- A refusal must be explained **in writing within 10 business days**, unless anti-money-laundering rules prevent the bank from giving reasons.

## What it includes

- Paying money in and withdrawing cash (in any EU member state).
- Payments, including online payments, direct debits, transfers and payments with a **debit** card. Credit cards and other credit products cannot be linked to it.

## Fees

- Running the account for **domestic** payments is **free**.
- The first **5 transfers** a month and the first **5 cash operations** at other providers' machines in Poland are free. After that the bank may charge, but not more than its most commonly applied fee of the last 12 months.
- Issuing the debit card is free (KNF guidance). Cross-border payments may cost extra; no other fees are allowed.

## Legal basis

Payment Services Act, chapter 7 (articles 59ia–59ih), in the consolidated text published in 2026. Further changes take effect in 2027; this guide covers the text in force when it was checked.
