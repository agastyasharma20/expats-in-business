PESEL is Poland's personal identification number. Many other steps, such as a [trusted profile](/guides/trusted-profile), need it.

## Two ways to get it

- **Automatically when you register your residence.** You get a PESEL number automatically if you register your residence in Poland for a stay of over 30 days.
- **By application.** If you cannot register your residence but are obliged by another law to have a PESEL, apply at any municipality (gmina) office.

## Applying

- Bring the application form and a document that confirms your identity and the data you enter in the form. You sign the form by hand.
- The form asks for the **legal basis**: the specific provision that obliges you to have a PESEL. If the application is incomplete you are asked to fix it within 7 days, otherwise it is left unprocessed.
- **In person:** since 1 January 2026, applying in person is required for foreigners who are **not** citizens of an EU, EFTA or EEA state or Switzerland, or qualifying UK citizens and their family members. Children born in Poland are exempt.
- The service is **free of charge**. The official accepts your application immediately. If there are grounds, you receive a notice of assignment at the municipality office where you applied; the form lets you choose a paper or an electronic notice.

## Which reason to give

There is no official list of reasons on the form or the service page. The Polish page says you must name the real legal basis, and that an institution which requires your PESEL (an employer or ZUS, a tax office, a university, the NFZ) should tell you which provision to cite. Ask that institution to name it and write it on the form.

## Legal basis

The Population Registration Act of 24 September 2010 (consolidated text Dz.U. 2026 poz. 384) and the Regulation of the Minister of Internal Affairs of 4 January 2012 on assigning PESEL numbers, which is still in force as amended. The service pages still cite an older consolidated text.
